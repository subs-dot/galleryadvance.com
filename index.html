An honest fit check

An advance is a tool. Here's when it's the right one.

A merchant cash advance trades speed and flexibility for a higher cost than a traditional bank loan. We'd rather you know that before you apply. A good funding relationship starts with the right product.

Often a good fit if you…

  • Need capital in days, not months
  • Have steady card sales or bank deposits
  • Have a short-term use with a clear return, like inventory, a new contract, or a busy season
  • Were turned down by a bank over time in business or credit

Probably not the right tool if…

  • You qualify for an SBA or bank loan and can wait for it
  • You need long-term financing for real estate
  • Revenue already falls short of current obligations
  • You'd be using it to cover another advance's payments

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Don't see your question? Email admin@galleryadvance.com and a funding specialist will walk you through it.

What exactly is a merchant cash advance?

A merchant cash advance gives your business a lump sum of working capital in exchange for a portion of its future receivables. Instead of borrowing money and paying interest, you sell a fixed amount of future sales at a discount, then remit that amount over time as your business brings in revenue.

Is a merchant cash advance a loan?

No. It's a commercial purchase of future receivables, which is why it's priced with a factor rate rather than an interest rate. Before you sign, your specialist will show you the exact total to be remitted, how remittances are collected, and any fees.

How much can my business receive?

Advances range from $10,000 to $3,000,000. Most offers fall between roughly half and one-and-a-half times your average monthly revenue, depending on your deposit history, time in business, and existing obligations.

How does repayment work?

Remittances are collected automatically from your business bank account, either as an agreed percentage of your receivables or as a fixed daily or weekly amount based on your average revenue. Agreements include a reconciliation provision, so if your revenue drops you can ask for remittances to be adjusted to reflect what you actually took in.

What do I need to apply?

A few details about your business (just email us to get started), your last three months of business bank statements, and a government-issued ID for each owner. Occasionally we'll ask for a voided business check or proof of ownership.

Do I need good credit to qualify?

Not necessarily. Our review focuses mainly on the health of your business, meaning your deposits, revenue consistency, and time in business. Owners with less-than-perfect personal credit are regularly considered.

Can I get additional funding later?

Yes. Once you've remitted a meaningful portion of your current advance and your account is in good standing, you may be eligible to renew, often for a larger amount.
